## Neutral facts

- The guidance distinguishes process, impact, and value-for-money evaluation because they answer different questions about implementation, effects, and costs or benefits.
- It recommends planning evaluation early and collecting evidence before, during, and after implementation when those stages matter to the evaluation question.
- Evaluation outputs should be timed around known decision points and should state limitations so findings are used responsibly.
- Monitoring and early learning can support timely adjustments, while impact and value-for-money judgments may require outcomes that emerge over a longer period.
- The guidance provides an evaluation structure and does not determine any particular crisis, organization, or regulation.
